## From where the six patterns came

Transcribed from `providers/redaction.py`'s `_PATTERN_RULES` at
`main 7e7d83b74e2903f2b83e95cf35d0115d4038df90` — the repository's own six
credential-detection patterns. **No rule was invented and none was adjusted to make
a test easier**; `§31.1`'s standing instruction that T20c and T21c are deliberate
rejections and MUST NOT be "fixed" by widening a rule is unchanged.

`plugins/evidence/rules.py` imports nothing from `providers/` — asserted by a test —
so the two rule sets can diverge, and the AI-05 one only moves by amendment.

## Ordering, and the one consequence

§30 numbers step 7 after step 1. It runs **before** record construction anyway, and
§14.10 records why: `EvidenceRecord.from_mapping` derives
`references.context[].value_digest` from the supplied `value` (§12.1), and §14.6 R2
forbids computing a digest over a value that was not admitted — "rejected before
hashing", in the contract's own words.

```text
Observable consequence: a record violating BOTH a shape rule and a published rule
reports the RULE. Both outcomes are REJECTED, nothing is stored, and no accepted
value, bound, digest or stored record differs by which reason comes first.
```

## Mutation / non-vacuity evidence

```text
each of the six rules, removed one at a time with the record otherwise well formed:
    before removal   the probe -> REJECTED (ADMISSION_RULE_VIOLATION)
    after removal    the same probe -> ACCEPTED
all six removed      every probe -> ACCEPTED
```

This is what distinguishes "the rule set refuses it" from "something refused it":
a suite asserting only `REJECTED` would pass against a system that rejected for a
different reason.
